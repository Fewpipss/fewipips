The Prop Trading Metrics Traders Should Track Every Week

6 min read Published November 25, 2026 Fewpips Team

(Twelve numbers, one weekly review, and the four that decide whether your account survives)

Short answer: Track twelve numbers every week: win rate, average winner, average loser, risk/reward, expectancy, maximum drawdown, consecutive losses, average trades per day, best session, worst session, rule violations, and profit factor.

Only four of them drive decisions: expectancy, maximum drawdown, consecutive losses, and rule violations. The other eight explain why those four move. Review them once a week, same day, same sheet.

Why Weekly Tracking Matters

Most traders check one number: profit. Green week, good. Red week, bad. That is not analysis. It is a mood.

Here is the logic. One week of P&L is mostly noise. The ingredients behind it (how often you win, how big, how big you lose, whether you followed your plan) are far steadier. Track those, and you spot a leak early. Ignore them and your first warning is a paused or closed account under the Fewpips trading rules.

More screen time will not fix this. A study of Brazilian equity-futures day traders found that 97% of those who persisted for more than 300 days lost money, with no evidence of learning from day trading alone. You can read the full paper, Day Trading for a Living?, on SSRN. Time does not teach you. Measuring does.

The Weekly Dashboard

  • Win rate: winners divided by total closed trades.
  • Average winner: gross profit divided by number of winners.
  • Average loser: gross loss divided by number of losers.
  • Risk/reward: average winner divided by average loser.
  • Expectancy: what the average trade is worth in dollars.
  • Maximum drawdown: the biggest drop from an equity peak this week, plus how far you sit above your Maximum Loss Limit floor.
  • Consecutive losses: your longest losing streak.
  • Average trades per day: closed trades divided by days traded.
  • Best session: your most profitable window (Asia, London, New York).
  • Worst session: your biggest bleeder.
  • Rule violations: every time you broke your plan or a platform rule.
  • Profit factor: gross profit divided by gross loss.

The Four Numbers That Matter

  • Expectancy is the only number that says whether your strategy pays. Win rate alone lies. A 35% win rate at 3:1 risk/reward beats a 60% win rate at 0.5:1. What a good prop trading strategy actually looks like shows the sizing math behind it.
  • Maximum drawdown matters because on every Fewpips account, the Maximum Loss Limit trails your highest equity and only moves up. Hit it, and the account closes immediately.
  • Consecutive losses are your tilt alarm, and what to do after three consecutive losing trades is the playbook for when it fires.
  • Rule violations are the simplest number on the sheet. The target is zero.

Everything else is context. Session data tells you when to stop trading. Trades per day tells you whether you are overtrading.

Set Your Weekly Tripwire First

Before any math, decide how much you can lose in a week before you stop. This is your own rule, not a platform rule. Measure it from the week's opening equity. On a $50,000 account, pick one:

  • 1.5% = $750
  • 2.5% = $1,250
  • 3.5% = $1,750

Hit it, close the platform, and finish the review. The 1-Step daily loss limit is 4%, so the 3.5% option leaves almost no room. A prop trading risk management plan built from scratch shows how to size every position inside whichever number you choose.

A Worked Example

Here is an illustrative week on that $50K account:

  • Trades: 20 closed, 9 winners, 11 losers
  • Win rate: 9 / 20 = 45%
  • Average winner: $2,700 / 9 = $300
  • Average loser: $1,650 / 11 = $150
  • Risk/reward: $300 / $150 = 2.0
  • Expectancy: (0.45 × $300) - (0.55 × $150) = $135 - $82.50 = $52.50 per trade
  • Check: 20 × $52.50 = $1,050, which matches $2,700 - $1,650
  • Profit factor: $2,700 / $1,650 = 1.64

Expectancy is your win rate times your average winner, minus your loss rate times your average loser. At 2:1 risk/reward, you only need to win 33.3% of the time to break even.

Watch Out

Do not log a winning trade as clean when it broke a rule. A trade held under 30 seconds, a hedge, or a trade idea that lost more than 2% of the starting balance is still a violation, even if it paid. Count it. Winners that break rules train the habit that ends accounts.

How This Connects to Platform Rules

Your dashboard is an early-warning system for two hard limits. On every account with a daily loss limit (Instant accounts have none), hitting it is a soft breach. The account pauses until the server reset, and a second hit within 7 days breaches and closes it. The Fewpips FAQ has the short version.

The Maximum Loss Limit is stricter. It never moves down after losses, so a rising consecutive-loss count is usually your first sign you are heading toward it.

How The Dashboard Adjusts as You Grow

Your numbers change as your account does. As equity climbs, the floor trails up with it, so recheck your cushion every week. Your tripwire dollars grow with the balance, because they are percentages.

Withdrawals need care. The Maximum Loss Limit does not drop when you withdraw, so leave enough equity above the floor. On funded accounts, you also need at least 25 closed positions at payout (50 on the Instant $200K and $500K accounts), so watch average trades per day. Frequently Asked Questions

What Is a Good Win Rate for Prop Trading?

There is no single good number. It depends on your risk/reward. At 1:1 you need above 50% to break even, and at 2:1 you need above 33.3%.

How Often Should I Review My Trading Stats?

Do a full review weekly and a quick glance at the end of each day. Do not change your strategy based on one week of data.

What Is a Good Profit Factor?

Anything above 1.0 means you made more than you lost. Many traders aim for 1.5 or higher, but a profit factor built on 15 trades means very little. Judge it over a month or more.

What Counts as a Rule Violation on a Prop Firm Account?

Hitting a loss limit counts, and so do prohibited behaviors such as hedging, positions opened and closed within 30 seconds, and losing more than 2% of the starting balance on one trade idea. Breaking your own plan counts too.
