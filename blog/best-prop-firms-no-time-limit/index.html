What "no time limit" actually means

A few years ago, most challenges gave you 30 or 60 days to pass. If you did not hit the target in time, the account failed even if you never broke a rule. That deadline pushed a lot of traders into forcing trades.

Most firms have now dropped that deadline. But "no time limit" is used loosely in marketing, so it helps to define it. A challenge is genuinely deadline-free only if all three of these are true:

  • No maximum number of days on either evaluation phase.
  • No minimum trading day rule that quietly forces you to trade inside a short window.
  • No inactivity rule that closes the account if you do not trade for a set number of days.

That last one catches people out. Some firms say "no time limit" but still breach your account if it sits idle for 30 days. That is a soft deadline in disguise.

Why deadline-free evaluations help you pass

The number one reason traders fail a challenge is not a bad strategy. It is pressure. A ticking clock makes you take trades you would normally skip. Remove the clock and you can wait for the setups that fit your plan.

That said, no time limit does not mean no rules. You still have to respect daily and maximum loss limits, any consistency rule, and the profit target. A deadline-free challenge just removes one source of pressure. It does not make the target free.

How to check a firm is genuine before you pay

Run this quick check on any firm that advertises no time limit:

  1. Search their rules page for "time limit," "minimum trading days" and "inactivity."
  2. Confirm there is no maximum day count on both phases.
  3. Confirm the inactivity rule, if any, gives you enough breathing room.
  4. Read the drawdown model. A trailing drawdown with no time limit can still be tough.
  5. Check the payout terms, not just the challenge terms.

Time pressure is only one part of the equation. A firm with no deadline but a harsh trailing drawdown can be harder than a firm with a soft deadline and a fair static drawdown.
