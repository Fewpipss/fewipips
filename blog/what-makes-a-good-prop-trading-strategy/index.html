What Does a Good Prop Trading Strategy Actually Look Like?

6 min read Published October 16, 2026 Fewpips Team

(Hint: it reads like a rulebook, not a hunch.)

Short answer: A good prop trading strategy is one you can write down as rules, size against a known drawdown, and survive being wrong with. It has defined entries, a defined exit when the idea fails, a measurable edge, a controlled trade count and a plan for bad days. It is not the strategy with the highest win rate. It is the one that still has an account after a bad week.

Why A Profitable Strategy Is Not Enough

Most traders ask, "Does my strategy make money?" On a prop account that is half the question. The real one is, "Does it make money without touching a limit on the way?"

Here is the logic. Prop firms judge the path, not the average. Almost every firm combines a maximum drawdown with a daily loss limit, and many make the drawdown trailing, so the floor rises as your equity does. A strategy with a great average and one ugly week still fails. The rules do not care how it ends up.

The wider evidence is not kind to winging it. A study of Brazilian equity futures found that 97% of individuals who day traded for more than 300 days lost money (Chague, De-Losso and Giovannetti). Effort alone fixed nothing. A defined process has to.

The Seven Traits of a Strategy That Survives

  • Defined entry criteria. If you cannot state each condition in one sentence, you are guessing. "It looks strong" is not a condition.
  • Defined invalidation. Know the price or event that proves you wrong before you click. The stop goes in with the trade.
  • A measurable risk/reward profile. Win rate, average win, average loss. Written down. Win 40% of the time at 1:2 and you still average 0.2R per trade.
  • Controlled frequency. Enough trades to build a record, not so many that you burn the daily limit by lunch. Fees and minimum-day rules punish both extremes.
  • Known maximum drawdown. Your worst peak-to-trough in testing. If you do not know it, the floor will tell you.
  • A clear no-trade condition. Major news, thin liquidity, a day you are tired. Name it. Skipping trades is part of the system.
  • Rules for losing streaks. Decide in advance what happens after three losses: stop, cut size, walk away. Decide it calm, not angry.

Why Your Personal-Account Strategy Does Not Carry Over

On a personal account you can hold a loser for three weeks, average down, hedge or size up to recover. Nobody stops you. The only limit is your own pain.

A prop account swaps that freedom for hard constraints. There is a floor you cannot cross, a daily cap on losses, and usually restrictions on hedging, news trading or per-trade risk. Rules differ by firm, so test your strategy against the exact set you plan to trade. A method that only works when you are allowed to be wrong for a long time is not a prop strategy.

Pick Your Risk Per Trade First

One decision sets everything else: how much you risk per trade. On a $50K account:

  • Conservative: 0.25% risk, $125 per trade. Slow growth, very long streaks survivable.
  • Balanced: 0.5% risk, $250 per trade.
  • Aggressive: 1% risk, $500 per trade. Four losses in a day hits a 4% daily limit.

A worked example

Say your account has a 9% maximum loss limit and a 4% daily limit.

  • Maximum loss limit: 9% of $50K = $4,500. Starting floor: $45,500.
  • Drawdown budget: half the limit, $2,250. That is a rule of thumb, not a firm rule.
  • Worst losing streak in your test sample: 9 trades.
  • Risk per trade: $2,250 ÷ 9 = $250, or 0.5%.
  • Daily stop: three losses is $750, well inside the 4% daily limit of $2,000.

In plain English: risk per trade = drawdown budget ÷ worst losing streak.

Watch out: The biggest mistake is sizing up after a loss to "get it back." That is how a 0.5% strategy turns into a 3% day. It is also how you land on the daily limit. Cut size after losses. Never raise it.

How The Math Changes as Your Account Moves

Recalculate whenever equity moves. Your budget comes from your distance to the floor, not from the number you started with.

Lose, and the floor stays put while your cushion shrinks, so your dollar risk shrinks with it. Win, and a trailing floor rises behind you, so redo the math instead of trusting the old cushion. Take a payout, and on Fewpips the floor still does not drop, so leave enough equity above it.

Losses make you smaller. Profit never lets you coast. That is why this approach is self-correcting.

Frequently Asked Questions

What Is a Good Risk-To-Reward Ratio for A Prop Firm Strategy?

There is no magic number. What matters is expectancy: win rate times average win, minus loss rate times average loss, after costs. A 1:2 ratio with a 40% win rate is positive. A 1:1 ratio with a 40% win rate is not.

Can I Use My Personal-Account Strategy On a Prop Account?

Only if it passes with the prop rules applied. Re-test it against a trailing floor, a daily loss limit and any hedging or news restrictions. If it breaks one, adjust it first.

How Many Trades Per Day Should a Prop Strategy Take?

There is no fixed count. Take enough to build a record, but few enough that a bad run cannot reach the daily limit. On Fewpips, positions opened and closed within 30 seconds are prohibited, so check your hold time before you build around scalping.

How Do I Know If a Prop Firm's Rules Suit My Strategy?

List your worst losing streak, your typical hold time and your average daily loss, then compare them with the firm's limits. The Fewpips FAQ answers the common rule questions in one place.
