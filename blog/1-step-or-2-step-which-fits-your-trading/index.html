1-Step or 2-Step? Match Your Fewpips Challenge to How You Actually Trade

8 min read Published October 16, 2026 Fewpips Team

(The real question isn't which one is "easier." It's which one matches how you actually trade.)

Short answer: If you trade tight, high-conviction setups and want to prove yourself once, the Fewpips 1-Step challenge, with a single 10% target, gets you funded faster. If you'd rather spread the proof across two smaller targets (8%, then 5%) and want FX leverage up to 1:100, the Fewpips 2-Step is the better fit. (All figures here are for the CFD challenges and follow the Fewpips trading rules.)

Neither is "the beginner one" or "the pro one." That's a myth. They're two different risk paths to the same funded account.

Want the plain side-by-side first? Start with our 1-Step vs 2-Step overview. This guide goes one level deeper: how each structure's risk math meets your trading style.

Why This Decision Actually Matters

Most traders pick a challenge type based on price or vibes. Whatever's cheaper, whatever a YouTube guy recommended last week. That's backwards.

A challenge structure is really just a risk template. You're not choosing a "program." You're choosing how big a target you need to hit, how much room you get, and how that room behaves while you trade. Get that mismatch wrong and you're not failing because you're a bad trader. You're failing because you picked a structure that fights your actual style.

A trader who scalps five setups a day and closes everything before lunch has completely different needs than someone running two swing positions a week. Put either in the wrong structure and you get accounts blown for reasons that have nothing to do with edge.

What the Data Says About Pass Rates

An analysis of more than 300,000 accounts across ten prop firms by fintech provider FPFX Tech, reported by Finance Magnates, found that only 14% of traders passed a challenge and just 7% ever reached a payout.

Whatever you make of those numbers, they are a reminder that a structure only helps if your risk per trade fits it. That is what the rest of this guide is about.

The One Decision to Make First

Before you look at pricing, ask how many phases you actually want to prove yourself over.

  • Conservative: You want staged proof in smaller bites: 8% first, then 5%. Go Fewpips 2-Step.
  • Balanced: You're confident but not reckless, and you're happy to prove yourself in one phase with disciplined sizing. Fewpips 1-Step.
  • Aggressive: You want the fastest route to a funded account and you already trade with tight risk control. Fewpips 1-Step.

That means "1-Step has the tighter drawdown" is only true on the smallest accounts. The real trade-off is different: one bigger target in a single phase versus two smaller ones, plus 1:30 versus 1:100 FX leverage.

A Worked Example: $50,000

1-Step math:

  • Profit target: 10% of $50,000 = $5,000
  • Daily loss limit: 4% of $50,000 = $2,000 in any single day
  • Maximum Loss Limit: 9% of $50,000 = $4,500, which puts the starting floor at $45,500

2-Step math:

  • Phase 1 target: 8% of $50,000 = $4,000
  • Phase 2 target: 5% of $50,000 = $2,500
  • Daily loss limit: 4% = $2,000
  • Maximum Loss Limit: 8% of $50,000 = $4,000, which puts the starting floor at $46,000

On a $50K account, the 1-Step asks for $5,000 of profit once, with $4,500 of room to be wrong. The 2-Step asks for $6,500 across two rounds, with $4,000 of room. So the 1-Step is not the "tighter" one here. It starts with $500 more room and a lower total target. What you take on instead is one higher hurdle in a single phase and lower FX leverage.

One note on scope: this guide covers the CFD challenges, which have no time limit. The Futures 1-Step challenge is a different product with its own rules and a 35-day limit.

Watch Out for This

The Maximum Loss Limit on both structures is trailing. It is tied to the highest equity your account has reached, so it is not a fixed line set on day one. As your equity climbs, the floor climbs with it, and it never moves back down.

Here is what that looks like on the $50K 1-Step. You start with a floor at $45,500. You run the account up to $52,000, and the floor moves to $47,320 (91% of your peak). If you then slip back to $50,000, the floor stays at $47,320, so you're now working with $2,680 of room, not $4,500. Giving back open profit is what quietly eats your buffer. Our post on how the trailing floor works walks through more examples, and static vs trailing drawdown explains why it trades differently.

The most common mistake is sizing positions off the starting-balance number instead of the room you actually have right now. Match your risk per trade to the current distance to your floor and to your daily limit. Remember the per-trade cap too: no single trade idea may lose more than 2% of your starting balance, which is $1,000 on a $50K account. Our guide to position sizing for prop firm challenges shows how.

The daily loss limit works from your start-of-day equity, and floating losses on open trades count, not just closed ones. For the exact rule wording, see the Fewpips trading rules.

How This Connects to Broader Platform Rules

Whichever structure you pick, it sits inside the same Fewpips guardrails. Two rules get mixed up with challenge rules often enough to be worth separating out:

  • The consistency rule works on funded accounts. On CFD funded accounts, no single trading day may exceed 40% of your total accumulated profit. On Futures-based accounts the threshold is 50% of the cycle's total profit. The rules page defines it for funded and Instant accounts, not for the 1-Step or 2-Step challenge itself. The consistency rule guide has worked examples.
  • The 3-minute rule defines what counts as an active trading day on funded, Instant and Futures accounts: at least one trade held open for three minutes or more. It is not a blanket rule for every challenge. What does apply as a prohibition is the 30-second rule: positions opened and closed within 30 seconds are treated as latency-arbitrage style trading; see prohibited trading, precisely defined.

Leverage also differs by instrument, not just by structure; the full matrix is in How Leverage Works on Fewpips.

How the Right Choice Changes as Your Account Grows

The right structure isn't fixed forever. A trader who starts on the 2-Step because they wanted smaller targets while learning the platform often moves to the 1-Step on their next purchase once they've internalized their own risk behavior.

Account size matters here too, and the table above shows why. On a $5K or $10K account the 2-Step gives you the wider percentage buffer. At $25K they match. From $50K up, the 1-Step gives you more. So the same trader can reasonably choose different structures at different sizes. Your own results from one challenge tell you a lot about what your next one should look like.
